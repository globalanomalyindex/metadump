Commercial product photograph of a pair of wireless over-ear headphones standing upright on a lawn of hyperreal electric green grass, shot from a low angle against a saturated cobalt blue sky with a few soft, glowing white cumulus clouds near the horizon. The headphones are made entirely of transparent blue-tinted acrylic and glossy clear vinyl: the headband is a single bent ribbon of polished blue acrylic with smooth rounded edges, the ear cups are thick translucent blue polycarbonate shells through which the faint silhouettes of the drivers and a small circuit board are visible, and the ear cushions are plump inflated pillows of clear blue vinyl with soft welded seams, like Y2K inflatable furniture. Exposure is pushed bright and slightly overexposed: specular highlights and the clouds bloom into blue-tinted white (#E8F4FF) with a soft halation around the acrylic edges, and every white in the frame carries a cool blue cast. The grass is impossibly vivid electric green (#3CFF2E), dewy and backlit, each blade crisp and slightly translucent at its tips. Frutiger Aero and early-2000s translucent tech aesthetic: glossy, liquid, optimistic, clean. Shot on a medium format camera, 80mm lens, f/5.6, headphones tack-sharp with the horizon softly out of focus, high dynamic range, studio-grade product clarity.

## Notes

1. The backlight sentence matters most. Light passing through the plastic is what makes the color read as inside the material. If outputs look like painted or opaque blue plastic, strengthen the sun-behind and caustics wording. Adding more color words won't fix it.
2. FLUX.2 has no negative prompt, so everything is phrased as what to show. If it drifts toward matte or opaque plastic, add "fully transparent, glass-clear" instead of writing "not opaque."
3. The hex codes are anchors that FLUX.2 follows fairly literally. If the grass goes flat radioactive neon, move #3CFF2E toward #2BE84A. If the whites go neutral gray, push #E8F4FF bluer.
