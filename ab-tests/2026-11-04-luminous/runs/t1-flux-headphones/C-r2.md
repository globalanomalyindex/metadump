## Notes

1. FLUX.2 doesn't take a negative prompt, so the prompt never says "not painted." It describes how the color behaves in the material instead: thick parts read darker, thin rims read brighter, overlapping layers read denser, and light passes through. If renders still come out as opaque blue plastic, move the "color lives inside the material" sentence up into the first paragraph, because FLUX.2 gives more weight to whatever comes first.
2. Each hex code is attached to a specific object, which is how FLUX.2 follows them most reliably. If the grass drifts toward yellow-green or the sky goes teal, those codes are the first thing to adjust.
3. The white plinth is there to give the blue spill and caustics a white surface to land on. If you want a purer Bliss-style shot, drop it and have the headphones rest directly on the grass. Use a 3:2 or 4:5 aspect ratio to keep the open fields of sky and grass.
