Mammoth Steak (bone-in, reverse-seared, carved at the table)

A note on the meat first. There is no legal, buyable mammoth meat. Permafrost carcasses exist, but the record shows dogs and scavengers eating them, and one researcher's attempt to fry old meat turned it to liquid. Elephant is the hardest stand-in to source in the US (it falls under federal wildlife permits, 50 CFR parts 13, 14 and 23), so skip it.

This recipe uses mammoth as the size and the story. The meat is a huge bone-in cut of bison (a tomahawk ribeye), the closest buyable game steak. Bison is sold online as whole ribeye, strip and tenderloin, shipped frozen. If you'd rather not hunt for game, a bone-in beef tomahawk of the same size works with the same method. Reindeer fillet is another stand-in. It is a smaller cut, so use the quick version in the notes.

Serves: 4 adults, with some left over for a second meal.

Ingredients

  • 1 bone-in bison (or beef) tomahawk ribeye, 3 lb / 1.4 kg, 2.5 to 3 inches / 6 to 7.5 cm thick. Ask the butcher for weight and thickness, and check both before you buy.
  • Kosher salt, about 1½ to 2 tsp (roughly ½ tsp per lb)
  • Coarse black pepper, 1 to 2 tsp (add it late)
  • Neutral oil with a high smoke point, about 1 tbsp (1 tsp per lb is enough)
  • Unsalted butter, 3 to 4 tbsp (1 to 2 tbsp per lb)
  • Garlic, 6 to 8 cloves, smashed
  • Fresh thyme or rosemary, 2 to 3 tbsp chopped, plus a few whole sprigs
  • Flaky salt for the table

Equipment: an instant-read or leave-in probe thermometer (this decides doneness), a sheet pan with a wire rack, a 12 to 14 inch cast-iron pan, tongs, a large carving board.

Method

1. Thaw, if frozen (1 to 3 days ahead)

Thaw in the refrigerator at 40°F (4°C) or below. Allow about 24 hours per 4 to 5 lb. A 3 lb cut needs about a day. Thaw fully before salting, since salt lead time starts after the thaw.

2. Trim and salt (24 to 48 hours ahead)

Trim any hard fat and silver skin. Much of the gamey taste sits in the fat and on the surface. Leave a thin cap, because you'll render it later.

Salt all over, including the edges and around the bone. Set the cut on the wire rack over the sheet pan, uncovered in the fridge, 24 to 48 hours. A cut this thick needs the long lead for the salt to reach the middle. The surface will look wet at first, then dry out. The 24 to 48 hour range is my reading of the thin-cut and roast guidance, not a published figure for this exact thickness.

3. Bring to the oven (1 hour ahead)

Take the steak out of the fridge about 1 hour before cooking. Pat the surface completely dry with paper towels. A damp surface steams and gives you a grey crust. Heat the oven to 250°F (120°C) and keep the steak on the rack.

4. Cook low (about 1½ to 2½ hours)

Insert the probe into the thickest part, away from the bone. Roast at 250°F until the core reads 115 to 120°F (46 to 49°C) for a medium-rare finish. For bison, the leaner of the two, stay at the low end. Plan on 90 to 150 minutes for a 3 inch cut, but go by the probe, not the clock. Low heat first gives an even pink band from edge to center, with little grey under the crust.

5. Render and sear (10 minutes)

Heat the cast-iron pan on high until it smokes lightly. Add the oil. Pepper goes on now, not earlier, or it scorches.

  1. Stand the steak on its fat edge with tongs for 3 to 6 minutes, until the fat is clear and crisp.
  2. Lay it down and sear 1½ to 2 minutes per side, until a deep brown crust forms.
  3. In the last minute, add the butter, garlic and herbs. Tilt the pan and baste continuously.

6. Rest (15 to 20 minutes, don't skip)

Move it to the board, loosely covered with foil. The core keeps climbing while the hot outside evens out. Expect a rise of 5 to 10°F more. A cut this size is closer to a roast and needs a long rest. Carve it straight off the heat and the juices run onto the board.

7. Check the core, then carve

Probe the center once more.

| Doneness | Final core |
|---|---|
| Rare | 120 to 125°F (49 to 52°C) |
| Medium-rare (target) | 130 to 135°F (54 to 57°C) |
| Medium | 140 to 145°F (60 to 63°C) |

Medium-rare sits below the 145°F (63°C) USDA whole-muscle figure. That is a taste choice, so if you want to follow the minimum, cook to 145°F and shorten the rest rise accordingly. Verify the current USDA figure yourself before you rely on it. Whole-muscle steak is contaminated on the surface, which is why the sear matters, and why you should probe the core and not the outside.

To carve at the table: cut the meat away from the bone in one piece, then slice across the grain into thick slices, about ½ to ¾ inch. Put the bone on the platter for show. Serve with a spoonful of the pan butter and flaky salt.

If something goes wrong

  • Core still low after the rest (under about 125°F): put the steak back on the rack in a 200°F (95°C) oven until the probe reads your target, then give it a quick 30 to 45 second sear on each side.
  • A wide grey band already formed: you seared too hot or too long. Shorten the sear next time. There is no fix for the meat in front of you, so slice it thin and serve it with the butter.
  • Surface greys instead of browning: it wasn't dry enough. Pat it again, or leave it uncovered in the fridge a few more hours.

Making it less gamey (pick one or more)

Gamey is a catch-all. Darker, redder meat reads gamier, and overcooking makes it stronger, particularly in lean bison. The fixes work on different layers:

  • Trim the fat, where a lot of the gamey taste lives (already in step 2).
  • Salt early (already in step 2).
  • Milk or buttermilk soak, 2 to 12 hours: adds a day of lead time. Rinse and dry before salting.
  • Acid marinade, 1 to 4 hours: brightens the surface only. A long soak softens it.
  • Butter, garlic and herbs (already in step 5). A splash of red wine and a few crushed juniper berries in the pan suit game.

Or lean into it: leave the fat on and skip the soak if the wild flavor is the point.

Serving ideas

Present the whole bone-in cut on a slate or board, carved in front of the table. Roasted potatoes or root vegetables, a bitter green salad, and a red wine pan sauce suit it. Cool leftovers within about 2 hours and refrigerate them. Cold slices make a good second meal in a sandwich.

Other sizes

  • Smaller bison or beef ribeye (1 to 1½ inch): salt 45 minutes to overnight, sear first at high heat for 2 to 3 minutes a side, then finish in a 350°F oven to 120 to 125°F core, rest 10 minutes.
  • More guests: count meat by weight (about 12 oz of bone-in meat per person) rather than one steak each, and get a bigger cut, or two.
