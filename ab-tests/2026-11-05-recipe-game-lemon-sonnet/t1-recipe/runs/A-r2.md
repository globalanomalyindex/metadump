# Mammoth Steak

A note first: mammoths have been extinct for thousands of years, and you can't buy mammoth meat. This recipe is for the nearest practical stand-in. Use a thick, well-marbled cut of **bison, beef ribeye, or elk**. These are lean-ish, strongly flavored, and suit the same cooking style: big, simple, and fire-forward.

**Serves:** 2 | **Prep:** 15 min (plus 40 min resting at room temperature) | **Cook:** 12 min

## Ingredients

- 2 thick steaks (bison ribeye or beef ribeye), 1.5 to 2 inches thick, about 12 oz (340 g) each
- 1 1/2 tsp coarse sea salt per steak, plus a pinch for finishing
- 1 tsp coarsely cracked black pepper
- 1 tbsp high-smoke-point oil (avocado or refined sunflower)
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter
- 4 garlic cloves, smashed
- 4 sprigs thyme
- 2 sprigs rosemary
- 1 tsp juniper berries, lightly crushed (optional, gives a wild, primal note)

## Method

1. **Dry and season.** Pat the steaks very dry with paper towels. Salt generously on all sides, including the edges. Let them sit uncovered at room temperature for 40 minutes. The surface dries further, which gives a better crust.
2. **Heat the pan.** Set a heavy cast-iron skillet over high heat for 5 minutes, until it just begins to smoke. Preheat the oven to 400°F (200°C) if your steaks are over 1.5 inches thick.
3. **Pepper and sear.** Add the pepper and rub it in. Add the oil to the pan, then lay in the steaks away from you. Don't move them for 2 to 3 minutes, until a deep brown crust forms. Flip and sear the second side for 2 minutes. Use tongs to sear the fat cap and edges for 30 seconds each.
4. **Baste.** Lower the heat to medium. Add the butter, garlic, thyme, rosemary, and juniper. When the butter foams, tilt the pan and spoon it over the steaks continuously for 2 to 3 minutes.
5. **Finish to temperature.** For thick cuts, move the pan to the oven for 3 to 5 minutes. Pull the steaks at these internal temperatures (they rise about 5°F while resting):
   - Rare: 120°F (49°C)
   - Medium-rare: 125-130°F (52-54°C), recommended
   - Medium: 135°F (57°C)

   Bison is leaner than beef and turns dry and tough quickly past medium, so don't go further.
6. **Rest.** Move the steaks to a board and spoon the pan butter over them. Rest 8 to 10 minutes, loosely tented with foil.
7. **Slice and serve.** Slice against the grain. Finish with a pinch of flaky salt and the herb butter from the pan.

## Serving suggestions

- Roasted root vegetables (parsnip, carrot, potato) with the pan drippings
- A simple red wine and shallot reduction
- Charred wild mushrooms
- A bold red wine, such as Syrah or Cabernet Sauvignon

## Tips

- Use a meat thermometer. It is the most reliable way to get the doneness you want.
- Don't crowd the pan. Cook one steak at a time if your skillet is small.
- For an open-fire version, cook over hot coals, sear 3 minutes per side, then move to indirect heat to finish.
