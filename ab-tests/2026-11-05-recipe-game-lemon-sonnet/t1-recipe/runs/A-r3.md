# Mammoth Steak

Mammoth is extinct, so this recipe assumes you have a legitimate cut. That could be a prehistoric find that has been properly vetted (permafrost meat is not safe to eat), a lab-grown product, or a stand-in. For a stand-in, use a thick, well-marbled beef ribeye or bison steak. They have the same dense, lean, slightly gamey character. Cook the real thing the way you would cook venison or bison: hot and fast, with a long rest, because it is lean and tough.

**Serves 2 | Prep 20 min (plus 1 hr to bring to room temperature, or overnight to marinate) | Cook 15 min**

## Ingredients

- 2 mammoth steaks (or ribeye or bison), 1.5 inches thick, about 12 oz (340 g) each
- 2 tbsp high-smoke-point fat (tallow, beef dripping or avocado oil)
- 1.5 tsp coarse sea salt
- 1 tsp cracked black pepper
- 4 garlic cloves, smashed
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme
- 2 sprigs rosemary
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter
- 6 juniper berries, lightly crushed (optional, for a wild, forest flavor)

**Optional marinade for tough or wild cuts:** 1/4 cup olive oil, 2 tbsp red wine vinegar, 1 tbsp crushed juniper, 2 cloves garlic, and 1 tsp salt. Marinate for 4 to 12 hours in the fridge.

## Method

1. **Temper the meat.** Take the steaks out of the fridge 45 to 60 minutes before cooking. Pat them very dry with paper towels. Dry surfaces brown, and wet ones steam.
2. **Season.** Rub both sides and the edges with salt and pepper.
3. **Heat the pan.** Use a cast-iron skillet over high heat for 3 to 4 minutes, until it just begins to smoke. Add the high-smoke-point fat.
4. **Sear.** Lay the steaks away from you. Don't move them for 3 to 4 minutes, until a deep brown crust forms. Flip and sear for another 3 minutes. Use tongs to sear the fat cap and edges for 30 seconds each.
5. **Baste.** Lower the heat to medium. Add the butter, garlic, thyme, rosemary and juniper. When the butter foams, tilt the pan and spoon it over the steaks continuously for 2 to 3 minutes.
6. **Check the temperature.** Pull the steaks at these internal temperatures, which will rise about 5 degrees F (3 C) while resting:
   - Rare: 120 F (49 C)
   - Medium-rare: 125-130 F (52-54 C), recommended
   - Medium: 135-140 F (57-60 C)

   Don't go past medium. Lean game meat turns dry and chewy.
7. **Rest.** Move the steaks to a warm plate or a rack and tent them loosely with foil for 8 to 10 minutes. Don't skip this. It lets the juices settle.
8. **Slice.** Cut against the grain into 1/2-inch slices. Finish with flaky salt and pour the pan juices over the top.

## Serving ideas

- Roasted root vegetables (parsnip, carrot, celeriac)
- Mashed potatoes or a wild mushroom and barley pilaf
- A sharp lingonberry or cranberry sauce
- A bold red wine: Syrah, Malbec or Cabernet

## Tips

- **Tenderness:** For a cut from a very old or tough animal, marinate it, or skip the steak method. Braise it low and slow for 3 to 4 hours instead.
- **Safety:** Cook wild or unfamiliar meat to at least medium. Check it for parasites. Ancient or thawed permafrost meat should never be eaten.
- **Leftovers:** Slice thin for sandwiches or tacos, and eat within 3 days.
