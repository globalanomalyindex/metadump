# Mammoth Steak

A note first: woolly mammoth went extinct thousands of years ago, so you can't buy it. Frozen mammoth meat has turned up in permafrost, but it's not safe to eat and not for sale. Mammoth is also a lean, red-meat animal, close to elephant, and the closest thing to it that you can cook is **a thick, lean cut of grass-fed beef (flat iron, hanger, or bavette) or bison**. The recipe below is built for that stand-in, using the techniques that suit a lean, dense, slightly gamey meat: a hard sear, a short rest, and a rich pan sauce.

**Serves 2 | Prep 15 min (plus 40 min to come to room temperature) | Cook 15 min**

## Ingredients

**Steak**
- 2 steaks, about 8 oz (225 g) each and 1 to 1.5 inches (3 to 4 cm) thick: bison sirloin, or beef flat iron or hanger
- 1 tbsp coarse kosher salt
- 1 tsp freshly cracked black pepper
- 1 tbsp high-smoke-point oil (avocado or refined sunflower)
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter
- 4 cloves garlic, smashed
- 3 sprigs thyme
- 1 sprig rosemary

**Juniper-red wine pan sauce**
- 1 shallot, finely diced
- 5 juniper berries, lightly crushed (optional, but they give a nice "ancient wilderness" note)
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) dry red wine
- 1/2 cup (120 ml) beef stock
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tbsp cold butter
- Salt to taste

## Method

1. **Season and temper.** Pat the steaks very dry. Salt them generously on all sides and leave them on a rack at room temperature for 40 minutes. Add the pepper just before cooking.
2. **Heat the pan.** Set a cast-iron skillet over high heat for 3 to 4 minutes, until it just starts to smoke. Add the oil.
3. **Sear.** Lay the steaks in away from you. Don't move them for 2 to 3 minutes, until a deep brown crust forms. Flip and sear the second side for 2 minutes.
4. **Baste.** Lower the heat to medium. Add the butter, garlic, thyme and rosemary. Tilt the pan and spoon the foaming butter over the steaks for 1 to 2 minutes.
5. **Check doneness.** Lean meats dry out quickly, so don't go past medium. Pull the steaks at these internal temperatures; they'll rise about 5 F (3 C) as they rest.
   - Rare: 120 F (49 C)
   - Medium-rare: 125 to 130 F (52 to 54 C), recommended
   - Medium: 135 F (57 C)
6. **Rest.** Move the steaks to a board and rest them for 8 to 10 minutes, loosely tented with foil.
7. **Make the sauce.** Pour off all but 1 tbsp of fat. Over medium heat, soften the shallot and juniper for 1 minute. Add the wine and scrape up the browned bits. Reduce by half, then add the stock and mustard and reduce again until it coats a spoon, about 4 minutes. Take the pan off the heat and swirl in the cold butter. Season with salt.
8. **Serve.** Slice the steaks against the grain, pour any resting juices into the sauce, and spoon it over the meat.

## Serving ideas
- Roasted root vegetables (parsnip, carrot, celeriac)
- Crushed potatoes with rosemary
- A bitter green salad with lemon to cut the richness

## Tips
- Lean meat needs a hot pan and a short cook. Overcooking is the main thing that goes wrong.
- Always slice against the grain, since these cuts have long, coarse fibres.
- For a stronger "wild" flavor, rub the steaks with a little crushed juniper and dried sage before searing.
