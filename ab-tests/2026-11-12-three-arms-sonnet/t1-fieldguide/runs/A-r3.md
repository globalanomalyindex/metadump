# Field Guide Entry: The Ember-Backed Dragon

***Draco carbonifer***
*Also called: cinder wyrm, hearth-serpent, the Slow Fire*

---

## At a Glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Family** | Draconidae (the true dragons) |
| **Length** | 9 to 14 m nose to tail-tip; the tail is nearly half of it |
| **Wingspan** | 16 to 22 m |
| **Weight** | 2 to 3 tonnes (hollow bones; heavier than a horse, lighter than it looks) |
| **Lifespan** | 300 to 400 years, perhaps more |
| **Status** | Rare. Not endangered, but only because few people live long enough to count them. |
| **Danger** | High when provoked, low otherwise. |

## Identification

The Ember-Backed Dragon is easiest to recognise by its **back**. The dorsal scales are matte charcoal, but they hold heat. After a hunt or a cold night's flight, the ridge glows a dull orange along the spine, like a banked fire. By dawn it has faded to black. In the field this is the surest mark. Other large reptiles may look similar from a distance, but none of them glow.

**Other marks:**

- **Head:** long and wedge-shaped, with two swept-back horns and a heavy brow ridge. The eyes are amber with a vertical pupil, and they narrow to a slit in bright light.
- **Wings:** leathery membranes stretched over four elongated finger bones. The wings are dark above and a pale rust colour below, which is useful when one passes overhead.
- **Forelimbs:** the dragon has four legs and two wings, not six limbs as some old bestiaries claim. The forelimbs are strong, and it can grip with them.
- **Underbelly:** broad, overlapping plates of dull gold. Males have deeper colouring.
- **Tail:** thick at the base, tapering to a spade-shaped tip. It is used as a rudder in flight and as a club on the ground.

## Similar Species

| Species | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|
| **Wyvern** | Only two legs; the wings *are* the forelimbs. Smaller, and aggressive without the dragon's patience. |
| **Marsh drake** | Under 4 m, olive green, lives in wetland. No glow, no horns. |
| **Cave wyrm** | Wingless, pale and nearly blind. Found underground only. |

## Range and Habitat

The Ember-Backed Dragon prefers **volcanic highlands and high, dry mountain country**. It looks for warm rock: lava fields, geothermal vents, south-facing cliffs that hold the sun's heat into the evening. It lairs in deep caves or old caldera rims, usually above the treeline, with a clear drop for launching into flight.

It keeps a **home range of about 200 to 400 square kilometres** and rarely leaves it. Territories are marked with scorched stone and claw-scored cliff faces, which are visible from below if you know what to look for.

## Behaviour

**Diet.** The dragon is a carnivore and an opportunist. It takes mountain goat, elk, wild cattle, and the occasional bear. A large meal can sustain it for weeks. It also collects metal, but it does not eat it, and the reason is not well understood.

**Daily rhythm.** It is mostly **crepuscular**, hunting at dusk and dawn. It spends midday basking on sun-warmed rock and the night curled around its heat-holding stones.

**Fire.** Flame comes from two glands at the base of the throat that mix fuel and a catalyst on exhale. It is a weapon of last resort, not a hunting tool. A dragon needs hours to recharge, so a dragon that has just breathed fire is a tired dragon. Most fire is a warning: a short, bright burst aimed past the intruder, not at them.

**Hoarding.** The dragon gathers bright metal (coins, blades, bells, cooking pots) and sleeps on it. Current theory is that metal retains and spreads heat evenly, so a hoard is a mattress and a radiator as much as a treasure. A dragon will notice a missing cup, though, and it will remember who took it.

**Sociality.** Solitary. Two adults meet only to mate, once or twice in a lifetime. A female lays one to three eggs in a heated nest, and the young stay with her for about ten years.

**Voice.** Mostly silent. The dragon communicates through low infrasound, felt in the chest more than heard. If your teeth hum and the birds go quiet, you are being addressed.

## Field Notes: Observing Safely

1. **Keep your distance.** Observe from at least 500 m, and from downwind if you can. A dragon's sense of smell is excellent.
2. **Read the posture.** A raised head and an open, relaxed wing mean curiosity or indifference. Lowered shoulders, a flattened neck and a twitching tail tip mean you should leave now, and calmly.
3. **Do not run.** Running triggers the chase instinct. Back away slowly with your hands visible.
4. **Do not touch the hoard.** Not even a single coin. This is the commonest cause of bad encounters.
5. **Go at dusk, with a spotting scope.** The ember glow makes the dragon easy to find on a darkening ridge, and it is the best time to see it wake.

## Notes on Folklore

Many cultures say a dragon guards its hoard out of greed. The evidence suggests otherwise: the metal is where it *sleeps*. The "maiden in the tower" stories probably trace to the dragon's habit of nesting in ruined towers and keeps, which hold heat well.
