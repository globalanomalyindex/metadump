# Ember Wyrm

**Draco ignivomus** (Hollis, 1871)
*Family Draconidae. Order Squamata, in the old arrangement.*

**Scope.** One species, the Ember Wyrm of the Carrick Fells. It is not the winged dragons of the southern coast, which are treated separately.

---

## Names

- **Common name:** Ember Wyrm. Fell shepherds say "burnt-jaw" or "old blackchin".
- **Scientific name:** *Draco ignivomus*. *Draco* is Latin for a large serpent, and *ignivomus* means "fire-vomiting". Hollis chose the epithet from what he was told. He never saw the display himself.
- **First record:** Hollis, 1871. He described it from a shed skin, two jaw plates and a shepherd's account at Skarrow Gap. No specimen was taken.
- **Synonyms:** *Draco carricensis* Pell, 1884 is the same animal, described from a juvenile and listed as a separate species. *Wyrmus niger* Ashdown, 1902 is a misidentified large monitor and does not belong here.

## Identification

Check the animal in this order. The first three marks can be seen at about 60 m.

1. **Jaw.** The scales along the lower jaw and throat are black, as if scorched. The black grows with age. Young animals have pale jaws, and animals over about 20 years have jaws black to the collar.
2. **Tail.** There are five to seven pale bands on a dark tail, and the band count stays fixed for life. Band count is the surest way to tell individuals apart.
3. **Build.** The body is low and heavy, held close to the ground on four short limbs. There are **no wings**. A ridge of raised scales runs from the nape to mid-back.
4. **Throat flash.** Seen only when the animal is alarmed, as a patch of bright orange skin under the chin that shows when the throat is inflated. A dull throat means the animal is calm.
5. **Feet.** Each foot has four claws and a fifth, smaller one set back. The tracks show this.

**Size.** Adults average 2.8 m from snout to tail tip. The longest reliably measured animal was 3.4 m, which is a little longer than the verified maximum for a Komodo dragon (3.13 m). Weight is about 150 kg, close to the Komodo's recorded 166 kg maximum, though the Wyrm is longer-bodied for the same weight. Standing beside a farm gate, an adult's shoulder reaches about the second bar.
*Hatchlings are about 40 cm and fit in two cupped hands.*

## Range and habitat

- **Range:** the Carrick Fells, from Skarrow Gap east to the Dunmere scree, at 400 to 900 m. Records above 1,000 m are rare and none are confirmed.
- **Habitat:** south-facing rock shelves with a wet gully or tarn nearby. Dens are in scree and under overhanging slabs.
- **Season:** seen on the open fell from April to October. From November to March it stays in the den and is very rarely seen.
- **Out of range:** a sighting below 300 m or west of the Carrick river is possible but unlikely, and should be recorded with extra care.

## Behaviour

- **Activity:** basks on warm rock in the morning and is on the move by mid-morning. It is inactive in rain and mist. It does not hunt at night, but a night call is reported (see Traces).
- **Diet:** carrion first. It finds a carcass by smell and may cover several kilometres between meals. It also ambushes sheep and deer on narrow trails. A large kill is followed by a fast of several weeks, so a fresh kill on the fell is a good clue that one is near.
- **Display:** when alarmed, it opens the jaws wide, inflates the throat and shows the orange flash. In older animals the gular glands release an oil that ignites in air, which blackens the jaw scales over the years. The display is a bluff, as with the frilled-neck lizard of Australia, which spreads a frill up to 30.5 cm across and runs rather than fights. The Wyrm prefers to retreat, and the flame carries about 2 m.
- **Lifecycle:** clutches of 12 to 30 eggs are laid in a mound of rotted bracken in June. Incubation is 6 to 9 months, so a nest found in spring is already in its second season. Lifespan is 25 to 30 years in the wild.

## Traces

- **Tracks:** broad, with four claw marks and a smaller fifth set back, and a tail drag line between them.
- **Shed skin:** found in rock cracks in late summer. The tail bands are visible on it, so the band count can be read from the skin alone.
- **Scorch lines:** a line of burnt grass 1 to 2 m long, on the open side of a trail. It is usually found beside a fresh kill.
- **Call:** a low, repeated note heard at dusk in September and October, which carries about a kilometre. Heard but never confirmed with a sighting.

## Lookalikes and cautions

- **Large monitor lizard (escaped captive):** also low-bodied, but with a pale throat, a single-colour tail and a yellow forked tongue. It has no black jaw and no throat flash.
- **Southern Fell Drake, *D. alatus*:** has wings. Any winged animal in the Fells is that species, not this one.
- **Cautions, by part:**
  - *Jaw and throat:* an orange flash means the animal is about to display. Back away and do not approach from the front.
  - *Tail:* it is used as a counterweight and a sweep. Keep clear of the rear quarters.
  - *Dens:* a dark opening under a slab with a smell of smoke is occupied.
  - *Carcasses:* do not approach a fresh kill. The animal will return to it.

## Notes and sources

- A two-headed juvenile was reported at Dunmere in 1893. The only record is a letter, with no skin and no drawing, and it is treated here as unconfirmed.
- Clutch size varies between sources (12 to 21 in some, about 30 in others). The figures above give the span.
- Komodo and frilled-neck figures are from Smithsonian National Zoo and Denver Zoo species pages. All Ember Wyrm figures are from the field journals of the Carrick Survey and are not checked against specimens.
- **Not yet known:** the call has never been tied to a sex or a season of breeding. A surveyor who can record it at the den would settle this.
