# Cloud-ridge Drake

***Anguidraco nubicola*** (Vael, 1871)

*Scope: one species, as recorded on the north escarpment of the Veshti Range. Other drakes of the genus are noted under Lookalikes.*

---

## Names

**Classification.** Class Reptilia; order Squamata; family Draconidae; genus *Anguidraco*.

**Etymology.** *Anguidraco*, "snake-dragon", for the long trunk and tail. *Nubicola*, "cloud-dweller", for the cloud forest it is found in. Local herders call it the *ash-jaw*, after the black staining on old animals.

**First record.** Vael's survey party, October 1871. They found a shed skin 5.1 m long on a basalt ledge at 1,900 m. The animal itself was not seen until the following season.

**Synonyms.** *Draco vaelii* (Hartmann, 1874) is the same animal, described from a juvenile and given its own species name. *Pyrosaurus ridgeri* in older texts mixes up two specimens and should be dropped.

## Identification

Check these in order. The first two can be seen from 200 m.

- **Wing.** A pale membrane held out on **seven** elongated ribs, so from below it shows as a fan with seven spokes. The forelimbs attach to its leading edge, as in the flying lizards (*Draco*), whose membrane is held by five to seven ribs. The wing glides. It does not flap.
- **Underside.** Pale grey from throat to vent. The back is dull slate with brown banding on the tail (9 to 11 bands). Colour is for breaking up the outline against rock and fog. Judge the animal by its shape.
- **Neck frill.** Folded and almost invisible at rest. Raised, it is about 70 cm across, roughly 1.4 times the length of the head. The frilled-neck lizard of northern Australia raises a frill up to 30.5 cm, so this one is more than twice as wide.
- **Feet.** Four legs, five claws on each foot. Hind claws are 9 to 12 cm along the curve.
- **Jaw.** On animals over about 20 years the scales along the lower jaw are black and glossy. Younger animals have none of this.

**Size.** Snout to tail tip, adults run 4.2 to 4.9 m. The longest verified Komodo dragon is 3.13 m, so this drake is about half again as long. Typical weight is near 210 kg, against a verified Komodo maximum of 166 kg. Wingspan with the membrane fully spread is about twice the body length (8 to 10 m). The tail is a little over half of total length.

## Range and habitat

**Range.** North escarpment of the Veshti Range, between the Kellan and Orsu valleys, at **1,400 to 2,300 m**. Seen from October to March. It is rare on the south face and is not recorded below 1,100 m. A single vagrant record on the Sarn plateau in January 1903 is thought to be a glide carried by storm winds.

**Habitat.** Cloud forest with large old trunks to launch from, and bare basalt ledges for basking. It dens in rock crevices above the fog line during the cold months.

**Seasonal movement.** It descends from the high ledges in October as the dry cold sets in, and returns above 2,000 m by April. It is rarely seen in the long wet season.

## Behavior

**Activity.** It basks on sun-warmed rock from mid-morning, then hunts in the afternoon and on foggy nights. This is a body that needs sun to work, but it also hunts in cold, damp air. It sleeps out the coldest weeks (December to January) in a den and does not feed then.

**Diet.** Mostly carrion, found by smell. The forked tongue samples the air before every move. The Komodo dragon is reported to find carrion from about 4 km, and the drake is believed to do something similar, though the figure is not measured. It also ambushes along game trails and can lie still for hours. After a large kill it may feed until its body is visibly swollen, then fast for weeks. A fresh carcass is the best place to wait for one.

**Glide.** Launches from a trunk at 15 to 25 m up and descends on a shallow angle, steering with the forelimbs. Landings are heavy.

**Display and defence.** In order: it raises the frill and gapes (the display makes it look much larger for a moment), then it runs, upright on the hind legs for short distances, then bites. Watch the jaw. A drake with its mouth open and frill up is bluffing and usually leaves. One with the frill folded and the head low is not.

**Fire.** Older animals release a volatile oil from glands at the throat. It ignites in air and is used only in display, as a short flash. It leaves the jaw scales black, which is how adults are aged. Ignition is not hunting behavior, and no kill has been recorded as burned.

## Lifecycle

- **Egg.** Clutch of 12 to 16, laid in a mound of leaf litter on a south-facing slope in May. Incubation takes about 8 months. A mound found in winter dates the season.
- **Hatchling.** Hatches in January. About 60 cm long and 450 g, small enough to hold in two hands. It climbs, hides in bark and does not glide until about its third year.
- **Juvenile.** Under 2 m, banding bright, no frill. This is the stage the synonym *Draco vaelii* was described from.
- **Adult.** Reached at 12 to 15 years. The longest-lived animal on record was marked on a ledge in 1938 and last seen in 1969.

## Traces

- **Shed skin.** Complete, whole-body, found on ledges after the October descent. A fresh one is soft and grey.
- **Tracks.** Five-toed, with a drag line from the tail in loose scree. The hind print is about 30 cm long.
- **Scorch.** A black streak on rock or trunk, 1 to 3 m long, from display fire. It is not a burned field.
- **Carrion.** Carcasses with the belly opened and the limbs torn off, left under trees.

## Lookalikes

| Species | Mark that separates it | How to see it |
|---|---|---|
| Large monitor (*Varanus*) | Forked tongue sampling the air; no membrane | Close range, on the ground |
| Flying lizard (*Draco*) | Smaller than 30 cm, with a membrane on 5 to 7 ribs | A glide between trunks |
| Wyvern-form drake (*A. bipes*, uncommon) | Two legs only, with the wing in place of the forelimbs | Leg count, from the side |

## Notes and sources

Vael recorded the range in 1871 but not the season. The season comes from Hartmann's 1874 notes and later from the herders' accounts. The herders put the descent in October and the return in April, and the records agree.

**Disputed.** Hartmann reported a length of 6.2 m. No later measurement exceeds 5 m, and the figure above is the best verified. His animal may have been measured with the shed skin stretched.

**Gap.** No one has recorded how the oil ignites, or how the drake avoids burning itself. No specimen has been dissected.

*Legend.* Sizes in metres are snout to tail tip. Comparison figures for Komodo and frilled-neck lizards are published measurements. Everything else is from field notes.
