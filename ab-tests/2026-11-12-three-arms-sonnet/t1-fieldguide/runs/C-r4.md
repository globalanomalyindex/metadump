## Slate-ridge Dragon
*Draco saxicola* (Latin *saxum*, rock, and *-cola*, dweller)

**Plate 14.** Wingless, low-bodied, four-limbed. Look for the **orange throat flash on a pale underside** and the **hind claw as long as your hand** (9 cm). Legend: see below the entry.

**Scale line.** About 3.4 m nose to tail-tip, which is 0.3 m longer than the largest verified Komodo dragon (3.13 m, 166 kg). Heavier in the body than that animal: reported adults run 180 to 220 kg. The open frill is 60 cm across, twice the width of the head as seen from the front.

### Identification
- **Shape:** heavy body held low, like a large monitor lizard, with a thick tail that works as the counterweight. No wings. A fold of skin around the neck stays flat until the animal is alarmed.
- **Marks, in the order you will see them at a distance:** (1) the tail has 7 to 9 pale bands on a slate ground; (2) an orange throat flash, visible only when the frill opens; (3) a crest of 11 to 13 raised scutes running from the nape to the shoulders; (4) hind claws 9 cm, curved, worn smooth on the underside.
- **Lookalike:** the ridge monitor (*Varanus*), about 1.6 m, is the only animal likely to be mistaken for it. It has an unbanded tail and no crest. If the tail is banded, it is not a monitor.

### Range and habitat
North-facing scree and ledge systems in the Karrow Valley and the ridges above it, 1,400 to 2,100 m. Seen October to March on the lower slopes; the rest of the year it keeps to den ground above 1,800 m. Not recorded below the tree line or on the south faces.

### Activity
Out by day. It basks on sun-warmed rock for an hour or two after first light before it moves, and hunts through the late morning. From December to February it is torpid in a rock den and is not seen, though the den mouth shows scrape marks.

### Diet
Mostly carrion, found by smell: it samples the air with a forked tongue and can follow a carcass several kilometres. It also ambushes on goat trails, waiting hours without moving. After a large meal it fasts for weeks, so a heavy, slow animal is a recently fed one.

### Lifecycle
Females dig a depression in a south-facing bank and lay 20 or so eggs (reports range from 12 to 30). Incubation is about nine months. Hatchlings are 40 cm, under 100 g, and climb to the ledges. They are banded more brightly than adults. Wild lifespan is not known, but may be about 30 years.

### What to watch for
- **Defence:** it will not charge first. The sequence is: frill open, mouth gaping, then an upright run on the hind legs. If pursued it may regurgitate its last meal to lighten itself. The bite is kept for when it is cornered, and it is serious.
- **Traces:** shed scales the size of a thumbnail, grey in shade and bronze in sun; a drag mark with a claw groove on each side; a roost where gold or worked stone lies mixed with bones.

### Notes and sources
First recorded by a survey party in the Karrow in autumn. The witness, a goatherd, put the animal at "twice the length of a mule." The party measured a shed skin at 3.4 m and noted that skins stretch, so the true length may be less. No adult has been weighed, so the weights above are estimates. Nobody has reported the throat colour in winter.

Comparison figures for the Komodo dragon are from the Smithsonian's National Zoo; the frill width comparison draws on the frilled lizard (Denver Zoo, frill up to 30.5 cm). Everything else about the Slate-ridge dragon is from the survey record.
