Mammoth Steak

Serves 2 | Active time 20 min | Total time about 1 hr 15 min (plus a short rest)

One honest note first: you can't buy mammoth meat. The animal has been extinct for roughly 4,000 years, and thawed permafrost meat is not safe to eat. The closest real-world analogues are lean, deep-flavored, slightly gamey red meats, so this recipe is written for those. Buy bison (my first pick), elk, venison loin, or a thick grass-fed beef steak. If you do land a mammoth, treat it like very lean game: don't overcook it, and don't skip the fat.

Ingredients

  • 2 thick steaks, 1.5 to 2 inches (4 to 5 cm), about 10 oz (280 g) each, bison, elk or venison
  • 1 tbsp coarse kosher salt
  • 1 tsp cracked black pepper
  • 1 tsp juniper berries, lightly crushed (optional, but very "ice age")
  • 1 tbsp neutral high-heat oil (avocado or grapeseed)
  • 3 tbsp butter, or 2 tbsp beef tallow plus 1 tbsp butter
  • 4 sprigs thyme, 2 sprigs rosemary
  • 4 garlic cloves, smashed, skins on
  • 1 tbsp flaky salt, to finish

Optional pan sauce: 1/3 cup (80 ml) red wine or stock, 1 tsp wholegrain mustard, 1 tbsp cold butter.

Method

  1. Dry-brine (45 min to overnight). Pat the steaks very dry. Rub with the kosher salt, pepper and juniper. Leave uncovered on a rack in the fridge, at least 45 minutes and up to overnight. Dry surface means better crust.
  2. Temper. Take the steaks out 30 minutes before cooking. Heat the oven to 250°F (120°C).
  3. Reverse sear, low and slow. Put the steaks on a rack over a tray and roast until the center reads 115°F (46°C) for medium-rare, about 25 to 35 minutes depending on thickness. Lean game goes from perfect to dry quickly, so check early.
  4. Sear hard. Heat a cast-iron pan until it just starts to smoke. Add the oil, then the steaks. Sear 60 to 90 seconds per side, pressing lightly, and sear the edges too.
  5. Baste. Drop the heat to medium. Add the butter, thyme, rosemary and garlic. Tilt the pan and spoon the foaming butter over the steaks for about 1 minute, until the center hits 125°F (52°C).
  6. Rest 8 to 10 minutes on a warm plate, loosely tented. The temperature will climb a few degrees.
  7. Optional sauce. Pour off most of the fat, then deglaze the pan with the wine or stock. Reduce by half, stir in the mustard, then swirl in the cold butter off the heat.
  8. Slice and serve. Cut against the grain into thick slices. Finish with flaky salt and spoon over the pan juices or sauce.

Doneness guide (pull temp, before the rest)

| Doneness | Pull at |
|---|---|
| Rare | 115°F / 46°C |
| Medium-rare (recommended) | 125°F / 52°C |
| Medium | 135°F / 57°C |

Avoid going past medium with game. It turns tough and livery.

Serve with

  • Roasted root vegetables (parsnip, carrot, beet) with a little honey
  • Mashed celeriac or potato
  • Wilted greens with garlic
  • A bold red: Syrah, Malbec or a Northern Rhône

Tips

  • A meat thermometer is the single biggest upgrade. Don't cook lean game by feel.
  • Add fat. Game is lean, so the butter or tallow baste matters.
  • Don't crowd the pan. Sear one steak at a time if your pan is small.
  • Leftovers make excellent steak sandwiches with horseradish and arugula.
